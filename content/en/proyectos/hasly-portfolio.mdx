## The brief

Hasly Luna is a professional photographer — sessions, weddings, events, product
— and she is my sister. There was no client and no fee: I wanted to build her a
real portfolio and, along the way, go deep into editorial design and animation,
which was where I had the least craft.

## The problem

A photography portfolio carries an uncomfortable tension: **the more the
interface shines, the less the photos do.** Every design gesture that draws the
eye is taking it away from the work that came to be shown.

At the same time, a photography site that browses like an ordinary website does
not work either — it feels cheap next to the images.

## The decision

**A one hundred percent neutral palette, no brand colour.** Not one. The hardest
decision in the project and the best one: in a photography portfolio the colour
has to come from the photos, and any interface accent competes with them.

So that navigation would feel considered without stealing the spotlight, I built
a **full-screen scrolling system by hand** with GSAP Observer instead of reaching
for a scroll library. Each section enters along a different vector — diagonal,
horizontal, vertical — and that variation is what makes moving through the site
feel alive rather than mechanical.

With eighty images in play, performance was part of the design: code split by
section, modern formats and deferred loading, with the cover rendered on the
server and the rest in separate chunks.

## The result

In production at **hasly.studio**, scoring **90 on mobile and 99 on desktop** in
PageSpeed despite loading a gallery of eighty photos in seven categories, with
filters and a full-screen view.

It also ships a complete discovery layer for AI agents — a content-negotiated
`llms.txt`, `.well-known`, WebMCP — that scores **83 out of 100** as an
agent-native site. It was my testbed for something I believe is about to stop
being optional.

Two lessons I carried into everything since. **GSAP Observer over smooth-scroll
libraries**: more control, one dependency fewer and a natural fit with the rest
of the animation ecosystem. And that **`prefers-reduced-motion` is designed in
from the start, not patched in at the end** — on a site that moves this much,
adding it later would have meant rebuilding it.
